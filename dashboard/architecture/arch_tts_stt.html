Emotional expression — why it matters and where we stand
Why emotions matter on sales calls
Property managers don't respond to robots.

Sales needs to laugh naturally at a client joke, slow down when sensing hesitation, sound genuinely warm on a cold call about a $50K proposal.

Flat, neutral TTS kills conversion. The voice is the product.
Inworld emotion support (primary)
Emotion tags: experimental, English only.

Context-aware prosody: Inworld's model understands conversational context and modulates tone accordingly — no manual tagging required for most cases.

ELO 1,577 reflects human judges preferring Inworld's delivery in blind tests. Expressiveness is baked into the model.
Kokoro emotion support (fallback)
No native emotion tags.

Neutral delivery — consistent but flat. Adequate for voicemails and informational turns. Will sound noticeably robotic on warm conversational moments.

This is the primary reason Inworld won over Kokoro as the main model.
Missed: Chatterbox Turbo [laugh][cough]
Chatterbox Turbo has native paralinguistic tags: [laugh], [chuckle], [cough], [sigh].

ELO 1,080 vs Inworld 1,577. Doesn't win on quality.

Interesting fallback-upgrade path: if Inworld costs become prohibitive at scale, Chatterbox + explicit emotion scripting is the natural v3 alternative.
